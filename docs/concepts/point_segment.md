# Point and Segment Rebuilt

## 1. Point analogue: nod

A **nod** is not defined by position. It is defined by survival under repeated distinction.

A nod answers the question:

> What remains stable enough to be addressed after a rez?

Thus a point is not a tiny object. It is a stable boundary of distinguishability.

## 2. Segment analogue: breath

A **breath** from nod `a` to nod `b` is not a piece of a pre-existing line. It is a directed availability from residue `a` to residue `b`.

A breath carries:

- start-nod,
- end-nod,
- internal tact pattern,
- allowed tests that observe its echo.

## 3. Length replacement

Veyra does not use length as primitive.

The first replacement is **pulse-count shadow**: close or compare a breath by how many one-tact pulses it emits under a chosen test.

Later replacements may include:

- resonance spectrum,
- fold-depth,
- obstruction count,
- phase drift,
- compression cost.

## 4. Segment equality replacement

Two breaths are not simply equal. They can be echo-equivalent under a test family.

Example:

- A coarse test may see two breaths as the same if they have the same start and end.
- A finer test may distinguish internal tact pattern.
- A resonance test may ignore order but detect prime decomposition of closure.

## 5. First construction

Given nods `a` and `b` and one tact `τ`, define:

`B(a,b;n)` = a breath from `a` to `b` with `n` tact registrations in the one-tact shadow.

This is allowed only as a first shadow notation. Internally, `B(a,b;n)` means a transfer whose closure emits mode `n_V` under the basic test.
